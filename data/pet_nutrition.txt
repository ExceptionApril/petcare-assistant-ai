# Pet Nutrition Guide: Feeding Schedules, Portions, and Safety

## Daily Caloric Needs and Portion Sizes

The amount of food your pet needs depends heavily on their species, breed, age, activity level, and whether they are spayed or neutered. It's crucial to measure food using a standard measuring cup or a kitchen scale rather than "eyeballing" it.

### Dogs
- **Puppies (under 6 months):** Require more calories for growth. Typically fed 3-4 times a day. Check the puppy formula bag for specific feeding amounts based on their expected adult weight.
- **Adult Dogs (Active):** May need 30-40 calories per pound of body weight per day.
- **Adult Dogs (Inactive/Senior):** May only need 20-30 calories per pound per day.
- **General Guideline:** A 50 lb adult dog typically eats between 2 to 2.5 cups of dry food per day, divided into two meals. Always refer to the feeding guide on the specific food packaging and adjust based on the dog's body condition score.

### Cats
- **Kittens (under 6 months):** Need high-calorie kitten food, often fed 3-4 times a day or free-fed if using dry food (though portion control is often better for monitoring).
- **Adult Cats:** An average 10 lb indoor cat requires about 200-250 calories per day. This usually equates to about 1/3 to 1/2 cup of dry food, or one 5.5 oz can of wet food per day, divided into two meals.
- **Weight Management:** Obesity is a major problem in indoor cats. If your cat is gaining weight, slightly reduce their portion size or switch to a lower-calorie formula.

### Rabbits
- **Hay:** The staple of a rabbit's diet. A rabbit should have unlimited access to high-quality grass hay (like Timothy, Orchard, or Brome) at all times. Hay should make up 80% of their diet.
- **Pellets:** A small amount of high-quality, plain Timothy-based pellets (about 1/4 cup per 5 lbs of body weight per day).
- **Fresh Greens:** About 1-2 cups of fresh, leafy greens per 2 lbs of body weight daily (e.g., romaine lettuce, cilantro, parsley, dandelion greens).

## Toxic and Unsafe Foods

Never feed the following to dogs or cats, as they are toxic and can be fatal:

- **Chocolate:** Contains theobromine, which is toxic. Dark chocolate and baking chocolate are the most dangerous.
- **Xylitol (Birch Sugar):** A sweetener found in many sugar-free gums, candies, and some peanut butters. It causes a massive insulin release leading to severe hypoglycemia and liver failure in dogs.
- **Grapes and Raisins:** Can cause acute kidney failure in dogs (and potentially cats). Even small amounts are dangerous.
- **Onions, Garlic, Leeks, and Chives:** These can damage red blood cells and cause anemia in cats and dogs (cats are particularly sensitive).
- **Macadamia Nuts:** Can cause weakness, tremors, and hyperthermia in dogs.
- **Avocado:** Contains persin, which can cause vomiting and diarrhea in dogs and cats, and is highly toxic to birds and rabbits.
- **Alcohol:** Even small amounts can cause severe poisoning.
- **Caffeine:** Found in coffee, tea, and energy drinks; it is highly toxic to pets.

## The Raw vs. Cooked Diet Debate

Feeding pets (especially dogs and cats) a raw food diet is a controversial topic.

- **Proponents of Raw Diets** argue that it mimics the ancestral diet, leading to shinier coats, healthier skin, cleaner teeth, and higher energy levels.
- **Veterinary Consensus (AVMA, FDA):** Most major veterinary organizations advise against feeding raw meat diets due to the high risk of bacterial contamination (like Salmonella, E. coli, and Listeria), which poses a health risk not only to the pet but also to the humans handling the food. Additionally, raw diets are often nutritionally unbalanced unless carefully formulated by a veterinary nutritionist.
- **Cooked Homemade Diets:** Can be a safer alternative if formulated properly, but they MUST follow a recipe designed by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist to ensure all necessary vitamins and minerals are included.

## Hydration Guidelines

Water is the most critical nutrient for all pets.

- **General Rule:** A healthy dog or cat should drink roughly 1 ounce of water per pound of body weight per day.
- **Cats and Hydration:** Cats are notoriously poor drinkers and are prone to urinary tract issues. Feeding wet food (which is about 70-80% water) is an excellent way to increase their hydration. Cat water fountains can also encourage drinking.
- Always provide access to clean, fresh water. Wash the water bowl daily to prevent slime build-up.
